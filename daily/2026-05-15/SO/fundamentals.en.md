# Fundamentals — SO as of 2026-05-15

## Executive summary
Southern Company is a high-quality regulated utility with stable, regulator-blessed earnings (Georgia Power, Alabama Power, gas distribution) running a heavy growth-capex cycle to serve Southeast load growth (datacenters) and Vogtle 3&4 nuclear, now in-service. Profitability and dividend coverage are intact, but free cash flow is deeply negative and balance-sheet leverage is rising as capex outpaces operating cash. Valuation at ~24x trailing / 19x forward P/E and ~13x EV/EBITDA is rich for a utility, reflecting the datacenter / AI-power growth narrative.

- Total Revenue: 2025 $29.55B vs 2024 $26.72B (+10.6% YoY); 2023 $25.25B; 2022 $29.28B. Three-year revenue CAGR (2022→2025) is roughly flat (~0.3%) because 2022 was inflated by fuel pass-through, but the cleaner two-year growth from the 2023 trough is ~+8.2% CAGR.

## Red flags
- Free cash flow deeply negative ($-2.94B in 2025) and trending the wrong way as capex jumps 42% — dividend and growth are entirely externally funded.
- Net Debt/EBITDA ~5.0x and Debt/Total-cap ~73% are at the high end for the peer group; further debt issuance ($6.8B net new in 2025) raises refinancing risk if rates stay elevated.
- Equity issuance ($1.62B in 2025; share count up ~1.7% YTD 2026) dilutes per-share metrics — common in utility capex cycles but worth monitoring.
- Net income 2025 down YoY (-1.4%) despite +10.6% revenue — interest expense (+18%) and D&A (+14.5%) are absorbing rate-base growth; EPS growth dependent on regulator-approved rate cases keeping pace with capex.
- Insider transactions over last 6 months are net selling by multiple senior officers (Georgia Power CEO, COO, CAO) — small in magnitude but no offsetting open-market buying.
- Valuation premium (~10–15% to peer multiples) leaves little margin for execution slip, regulator pushback on Vogtle cost recovery, or softer-than-expected datacenter load build.
- Payout ratio 75.7% on earnings is high; combined with negative FCF, dividend growth pace likely modest.
- JPMorgan trimmed stake -29.8% in Q1 2026 — one large institutional reduction worth noting.
