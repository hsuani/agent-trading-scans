# Bear case round 1 — ETN

## Thesis (1 sentence)
ETN trades at a 40x trailing / 26x forward P/E and 28x EV/EBITDA on a 4-year-extended transformer/switchgear cycle whose order growth has already been priced in — with insiders dumping $131M into strength, cash halved to $803M, tangible book negative, and KeyBanc itself flagging input-cost margin pressure, the asymmetry has flipped to mean-reversion risk.

## Demand / market risks
- **Cycle-top crowding**: Bloomberg (5/12) explicitly called out ETN inside the "AI-mania industrials behaving like chip stocks" cohort — a classic late-cycle narrative tag that historically precedes multiple compression once order growth merely *decelerates* (not declines).
- **4-year transformer lead times are the peak indicator, not a moat**: 128-week standard / 144-week GSU / 3–5yr HV substations is what cycle tops look like in capital equipment. Q1 data-center orders +240% YoY is unrepeatable; the comp wall in H2'26 / 2027 is brutal.
- **Hyperscaler concentration**: MSFT/GOOGL/META/AMZN capex is the marginal buyer. The 2026 capex prints are already "digested" per news desk — any guide-down from a single hyperscaler hits ETN's order book directly.
- **Competitive supply catching up**: Hitachi (~$1B) and Siemens ($421M NC plant) capacity is landing 2026–2028. Powell (POWL), GEV, and Chinese MV switchgear entrants are all expanding into the same pull — pricing power has a defined half-life.
- **End-market saturation risk**: S&P projects datacenter share of US electricity rises 4%→9% by 2030; the back half of that S-curve is where order *growth* (not levels) rolls.

## Margin / cashflow risks
- **KeyBanc itself flagged input-cost margin pressure** — grain-oriented electrical steel and copper constraints persist; even the bull desk acknowledges margin risk.
- **Gross margin already compressed 60bps in 2025** (38.2% → 37.6%) — at a cycle high. The decompression has started.
- **FCF/NI slipped to 0.87** (vs 0.93 prior, vs 0.90 "healthy" bar) — working-capital build on backlog is not free cash.
- **Capex/sales up 13.7% YoY to $919M** — capacity additions into a potentially peaking cycle is the classic capital-cycle trap.
- **Current ratio 1.19, quick ratio 0.69** — tighter than industrial-average, current liabs +$1.5B YoY.

## Balance sheet risks
- **Cash halved**: $2.08B → $0.80B in one year. Net debt rose to $9.27B (+$0.67B).
- **Tangible book is negative ($-1.4B)** — entire equity cushion is goodwill/intangibles from Cooper. Zero asset margin of safety on impairment.
- **$1.06B new debt issued in 2025** alongside $1.86B buybacks at 40x P/E — borrowing to retire stock at peak multiple is value-destructive if multiples compress.
- **D/E 110%** — not catastrophic, but no dry powder for downturn-defensive M&A.

## Valuation risk
- **Bear: P/E 22x (industrial sector median) × FY26 EPS $15.70 = $345 → −15.4%** from $408.10.
- **Base bear: EV/EBITDA 18x × $6.18B TTM EBITDA − $9.27B net debt = $102B equity / 388.3M sh = $263 → −35.5%**.
- **Worst: cycle-peak rerate to 14x (HUBB/EMR style) × $14 cyclically-adjusted EPS = $196 → −52%**.
- Forward P/E of 26x demands ~$15.70 FY+1 EPS — a +50% earnings ramp from $10.45 trailing. Any miss and the 40x trailing is naked.
- **P/FCF 44.6x, EV/Revenue 6.30x, P/Sales 5.56x** — every cash-based multiple is industrial-extreme.

## Competitive / regulatory
- Chinese MV switchgear and Korean transformer entrants targeting the US capacity gap; trade-policy shifts could either help or hurt but introduce dispersion.
- Utility rate cases pushing back on AI datacenter cost-shifting (multi-state regulatory friction emerging) could slow the $1.4T capex realisation.
- Permitting/interconnect queues are the bottleneck, not equipment — meaning order *deliveries* could slip vs bookings, hitting revenue recognition.
